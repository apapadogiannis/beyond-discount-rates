\begin{abstract}
This paper introduces a dual-risk valuation framework that extends traditional discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis by decomposing expected return into a market-based cost of capital and a belief-driven gain of capital. By embedding expectation error directly into the DCF identity, the framework captures return premiums that arise from the persistent misestimation of future cash flows. We derive closed-form valuation expressions integrating both return components and introduce an effective hurdle rate for unified discounting. For empirical implementation, the model constructs two price-based proxies for the gain of capital: a realized gain, which recovers the theoretical gain exactly, and an implied gain, its valuation-space counterpart, which we prove to be exactly proportional to it. Applied to U.S.\ equity indices (SPY, QQQ, IWM) from 2000 to 2023, the realized gain of capital is large, persistent, and regime-dependent, exhibiting decade-scale sign regimes that no constant discount rate can generate. This framework provides a principled method for financial practitioners to dynamically adjust project hurdle rates, diagnose valuation asymmetries, and systematically incorporate expectation risk into capital budgeting decisions.
\end{abstract}
